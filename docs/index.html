Instrument Guide

How to interact with the Spectral Lattice.

The Canvas

The painting is divided into 16 slices. Each slice represents a harmonic piece of a musical tone. Tap or hold any slice to sound it. When all 16 are playing, the complete spectrum is reconstructed.

Side B: Tuning

In INTERACT mode, flip any card by clicking the arrow in the top right. This reveals Side B (the oscilloscope). Here, you can physically tune that specific pad by dragging the Cents slider, allowing for microtonal exploration.

Modes

  • INTERACT: Manually toggle slices on or off. Great for static ambient soundscapes and manual tuning.
  • STILL: Tap slices to play them momentarily like drum pads or a keyboard.
  • MOTION: A generative mode where the painting comes alive and plays itself autonomously.

Controls & Automation

  • GRAVITY: Adjusts the foundational base pitch of the entire painting.
  • TEXTURE: Shifts the harmonic richness, morphing the sound from hollow to bright.
  • MORPH: Automatically shifts the TEXTURE slider over time.
  • DRIFT (Interact Mode): Automatically and organically drifts the tuning of every slice, creating a swirling, detuned chorus effect.
  • HARMONIZE (Motion Mode): Forces the autonomous sequences into a pleasing musical scale instead of dissonant mathematics.

Helgoland Spectral Lattice

An interactive audio-visual instrument after Arthur Segal's painting Helgoland (1923).

Developed for the Sounding Collection Workshop.

The piece follows Segal's principle of Gleichwertigkeit — Equivalence: no element holds hierarchy over another. A single harmonic tone is decomposed into sixteen spectral slices. Only with all sixteen sounding together is the complete waveform reconstructed.

Creator
